Border checkpoints face identity fraud in the form of forged, digitally altered, expired and impersonated travel documents, together with morphed and synthetic facial images. Most screening tools return a binary authenticity decision. That does not tell the officer what failed, where it failed, or whether the record of what the officer was shown can later be verified. This work presents DRISHTI, a portable decision-support system that combines MRZ and ICAO Doc 9303 validation, document forensics, live-capture face verification with presentation-attack detection, cross-document consistency checks and a permissioned blockchain audit layer. Documents pass through an adaptive three-tier cascade in which deterministic checks run first and computationally heavy models run only when risk warrants, with randomised deep audits applied to a sample of low-risk documents. Each inconsistency becomes an evidence flag with a weight and a location on the document, and a risk engine aggregates the flags into Low, Review or High bands. A self-hosted language model summarises the flags for the officer, and a rule-based verifier rejects any summary containing a value absent from the flag data. Sensitive data remains in encrypted offchain storage, while Merkle-batched SHA-256 hashes of screening events, model versions and officer overrides are anchored to a permissioned ledger, giving agencies a tamper-evident audit trail. The system supports the officer's judgment and does not replace (Sanskrit for "vision") it. Keywords: document forensics, MRZ validation, morph attack detection, explainable risk scoring, permissioned blockchain, Merkle batching, human-in-the-loop, border security 1. Problem and Design Principles Manual inspection and disconnected checks lengthen processing time, cause congestion and make outcomes depend on individual officer expertise. DRISHTI follows four principles: - Inexpensive checks run first, and deep models run on demand. - Every flag carries evidence and a location. - Only hashes reach the ledger, never identity data. - The officer makes the final decision. 2. Screening Pipeline An officer scans or uploads a passport, visa or ID. A quality gate corrects alignment, checks for blur and glare, and returns unusable images for retake. Tier 0: Deterministic gate (target under 0.3 s) - MRZ parsing and ICAO Doc 9303 check-digit validation - Date, format and expiry rules - Watchlist, stolen-document and document-number history lookup - A hard failure exits directly to High Risk. For example, if the MRZ date-of-birth check digit is expected as 4 and found as 7, the case exits in under one second with the field highlighted. Tier 1: Light analysis (target 0.5 to 1 s) - OCR-to-MRZ field comparison - Barcode/QR decoding against printed fields - Template alignment of layout, fonts and text positions - Face detection, alignment and embedding, matched against a live capture with presentation-attack detection Tier 2: Deep analysis (target 1 to 2 s, GPU) - Tamper localisation network - Error-level, noise-residual and copy-move forensics - Stamp analysis - Morph and deepfake classifiers - Anomaly detector for unseen forgery types - Triggered when the Tier 1 partial score is ambiguous, the profile is high-risk, or the case is drawn for a randomised audit. The random audit stops an adversary who learns what passes Tier 1 from tuning forgeries to it. Cross-document verification - Names compared with transliteration tolerance - Passport number, validity windows and face consistency - For visas, MRZ data compared with the visible printed text History layer - Verified outcomes from earlier screenings enter as a bounded, decaying prior - No permanent identity-level suspicion - Current evidence remains the primary basis for every decision 3. Risk Scoring and Explainability The risk score is R = ? (w? · F?) + hard rules, where F? are evidence flags and w? their weights. R is mapped to Low Risk, Review and High Risk. Weights and thresholds are calibrated on labelled validation data and versioned, so policy changes are traceable. The Review band routes borderline cases to a human instead of rejecting them, which limits false alarms on genuine travellers. The dashboard shows each fired flag, its location as an overlay on the document, and its reason. A self-hosted language model converts the structured flags into a two-to-three-sentence summary, and a code-based verifier checks that every number, field name and date in the summary appears in the flag data. The model narrates and never decides, and no data leaves the agency's environment. 4. Threat Model The design assumes adaptive adversaries who probe the system and tailor forgeries to it. Countermeasures include layered detectors that must all be defeated at once, ensembles, non-ML deterministic checks, randomised deep audits, anomaly detection for unseen attack types, periodic retraining and red-team testing. 5. Permissioned Audit Layer Images, facial data, biometric templates and full OCR records stay in encrypted off-chain storage under role-based access. The ledger records only: - SHA-256 hashes of documents and screening records - Audit ID and timestamp - Risk score and outcome - Model and ruleset versions - Status transitions and officer actions, including overrides Events are batched into Merkle trees, so ledger writes scale across checkpoints while each event remains individually verifiable. Because overrides are ledgered too, the trail covers human decisions as well as automated ones. On a permissioned network such as Hyperledger Fabric, participating agencies can independently verify that a record has not been altered, and digital signatures establish the authenticity of each event. 6. Implementation and Scalability The MVP is modular: React and Tailwind CSS (dashboard), FastAPI (orchestration), Redis queue with stateless workers, Python with OpenCV, PyTorch, Tesseract/EasyOCR and InsightFace/FaceNet-class embeddings, PostgreSQL (operational data), encrypted S3-compatible storage (evidence), Hyperledger Fabric (audit) and Docker. The design is API-first, with mock watchlist connectors now and live connectors later, and it accommodates RFID/NFC readers for ePassport verification and UV or high-resolution scanners. Tiers 0 and 1 can run at the checkpoint for a fast preliminary verdict and tolerance of poor connectivity, while Tier 2 and the ledger run centrally. A queue absorbs load peaks, and a batched GPU service serves all sites. 7. Validation Plan The deterministic core follows the published ICAO Doc 9303 specification. Development uses public and synthetic datasets (MIDV-500, MIDV-2020, DocTamper, SIDTD) and published morph generators, so real passports are not required. Per-module metrics will be reported on held-out data: - Field-level extraction accuracy, plus deliberately corrupted cases that must all be caught - Tamper catch rate, false-alert rate and overlap between flagged and actual edited regions - False-accept and false-reject rates for face matching, reported per demographic group (NISTIR 8280) - Missed morph attacks versus falsely flagged genuine photographs (NIST FATE MORPH approach) - Per-document latency and throughput under concurrent load 8. Responsible Use and Impact Officers decide, particularly for adverse outcomes. Documents and faces are encrypted, and retention is configurable to agency legal requirements, including India's DPDP Act, 2023. Officers receive scored, localised evidence in place of unaided inspection. Agencies apply identical checks and thresholds at every checkpoint. Investigators can link repeat faces and reused document numbers across sites. Genuine travellers pass quickly, and borderline cases receive human review. The system supports action against identity fraud, human trafficking and cross-border crime and aligns with IVFRT modernization. 9. Roadmap - Core platform: upload, OCR and MRZ extraction, rule validation, face matching, risk engine, officer dashboard and hashed audit log. - Deeper detection: tamper localisation, morph and deepfake classifiers, cross-document checks and multiple-identity search. - Integration: live watchlist and stolen-document connectors, ePassport chip verification, permissioned ledger deployment and a pilot.
